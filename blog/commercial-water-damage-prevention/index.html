Commercial Water Damage Prevention in Woodbury, MN

Home / Blog / Commercial Water Damage Prevention in Woodbury, MN

Water damage is one of the most costly risks facing commercial property owners and managers in Woodbury, MN. Restoration is only the direct cost, because a commercial loss also brings potential liability exposure, displaced employees, lost inventory, and interrupted business. It is much cheaper to prevent a loss than to react to one with restoration. Below are the most effective ways to keep water damage away from a commercial property in Woodbury.

Building Maintenance Program

Preventing water damage begins with maintaining the building on a complete, organized program. Inspect the building envelope, fire suppression systems, HVAC equipment, plumbing, and roofing on a regular schedule. If damage ever happens, a documented history of inspections and repairs shows due diligence, helps shield you from liability, and supports your insurance claim.

Systems That Detect Water Early

Commercial water detection systems provide early warning of leaks before they cause significant damage. Today's equipment is able to monitor supply lines, water heaters, places that tend to flood, and HVAC condensate lines. On detecting a leak, certain systems close the water supply automatically. The investment in detection technology typically pays for itself many times over by preventing even one significant water damage event.

Have an Emergency Plan Ready

Plan ahead for a water emergency at your commercial property by listing emergency contacts for the restoration contractor (Woodbury Restoration, (760) 542-1883), property management, and your insurance agent, training staff on the main water shutoff procedure, assigning employee roles and duties for a water event, setting procedures that protect inventory and critical documents, and agreeing on how to communicate with stakeholders and tenants.

Insurance Points to Check

Once a year, sit down with your agent and go over the commercial property policy. Look for three things: business interruption coverage, building and contents limits that are adequate, and endorsements covering equipment breakdown as well as backup of drains and sewers. Comprehensive coverage raises premiums by a relatively modest amount, so compare that with the cost of being underinsured.
