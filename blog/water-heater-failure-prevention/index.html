Water Heater Maintenance Guide in Woodbury, MN

Home / Blog / Preventing Water Heater Floods in Woodbury, MN

Water heater failures are one of the most common causes of residential water damage in Woodbury, MN. When a typical 40 to 50 gallon tank lets go all at once, every gallon can be on your floor within minutes, and the harm to walls, flooring, and stored items can run into the thousands of dollars. Spotting trouble early and taking a few preventive steps is how you stay clear of an expensive emergency like this.

How to Tell a Water Heater Is Failing

A water heater close to failing usually gives notice: the hot taps run rust-colored, the tank pops or rumbles while heating, rust or corrosion shows on the outside, moisture or puddles collect at the base, hot water runs out sooner, the relief valve drips or discharges often, or the unit is past the 8 to 12 year lifespan typical of tank-style models.

Preventive Maintenance

A maintained heater lives longer and is less likely to fail, so once a year, flush the tank and get the built-up sediment out. Every 2 to 3 years, inspect the anode rod, and put in a new one if corrosion is significant. Test the temperature and pressure relief valve annually. Look over the supply connections for signs of wear or corrosion. Leave open space around the unit so it can be inspected easily. A leak detection sensor placed by the base is worth considering.

Is It Time for a New Water Heater?

The most effective way to prevent a catastrophic water heater failure is proactive replacement before the unit fails. If your water heater is over 10 years old, showing signs of corrosion, or requiring frequent repairs, replacing it now prevents a future flood. The cost of a planned replacement is far less than the cost of emergency water damage restoration. Going tankless is worth a look, since it removes the risk of a tank failure altogether.
