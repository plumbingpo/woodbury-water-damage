Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. It is not the repair. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. Our Woodbury crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. Done well, it shrinks the repair that follows and sometimes removes the need for one.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. Each is its own phase of the job. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Containment, using plastic barriers and controlled airflow so moisture and contaminants stay out of clean rooms. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

Why Hire a Professional for Water Damage Mitigation?

Mitigation is a series of judgment calls made quickly. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Should the toe kick under the cabinets be pulled? Each decision rests on meter readings and our training, never on whether something seems dry to the eye or hand.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Owners are required by most policies to take reasonable steps against further damage, and the insurer will ask for evidence. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Mitigation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.
  • Flooring is carrying moisture past the doorway to an adjoining room.
  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • After a leak, the baseboards have swollen or started separating from the wall.
  • A plumber fixed the pipe but nobody has dealt with the water.
  • Windows keep fogging and the air feels humid days after the water loss.
  • Wet materials have sat for a day and a musty smell is starting.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
  • Never switch the furnace fully off in freezing weather, since pipes can freeze and split.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Trade rubber washer hoses for braided steel, and renew them roughly every five years.
