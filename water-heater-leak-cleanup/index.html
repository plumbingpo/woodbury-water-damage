Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Woodbury: What to Know

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

In Woodbury, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWater from the tank can scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • Flex connectors or inlet and outlet fittings at the top of the heater that have corroded.
  • The relief valve for temperature and pressure opening, or failing, and letting water out.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Stored Belongings

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Time that passed with the tank still refilling, up to the point the supply was shut.
  • If water got to the flooring of adjoining rooms, and the kind of flooring involved.
  • Amount of contents affected, since wet belongings have to be relocated, dried or discarded.
  • The amount of wall drywall, ceiling drywall or insulation that has to be removed.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Remove Standing Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

Remove Wet Material

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

Dry and Monitor

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for the 3 to 5 days drying usually takes. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.
