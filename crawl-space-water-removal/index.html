Crawl Space Water Removal in Woodbury

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

Warning Signs

  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls
  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy
  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Weakened Floor Framing

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Contaminated Water

Water under a house is often not clean. Groundwater full of soil and waste, or a drain leak, counts as Category 2 or 3, which calls for protective equipment and disinfecting.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

What We Do

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.
