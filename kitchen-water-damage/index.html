Kitchen Water Damage in Woodbury

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. Our Woodbury crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

What It Looks Like

  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking

What We Do

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Remove Only Damage

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Refrigerator Water Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

The Dishwasher Cavity

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.
