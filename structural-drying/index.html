Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Woodbury Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Tell your restoration technician immediately if you notice new wet areas or odors during drying
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

When to Call for Structural Drying in Woodbury

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak

Structural Drying, Step by Step

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Equipment Deployment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. Temperature and airflow are optimized for the specific materials being dried.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

A technician comes back every day, reads moisture at each monitoring point, moves equipment as needed and logs the progress. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

4

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.
