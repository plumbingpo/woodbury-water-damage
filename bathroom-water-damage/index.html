Bathroom Water Damage: What Woodbury Homeowners Should Know

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. Our Woodbury team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

Warning Signs

  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Slow Vanity Drips

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Trapped Steam

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Damage Downstairs

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.
