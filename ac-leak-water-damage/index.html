Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Woodbury

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Right Now

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ACNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Call Both TradesCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Poorly connected drain piping, which lets water out before it reaches the outlet.
  • A missing or failed float switch that should have shut the system off.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Closet Walls and Platform

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Ceilings Under the Unit

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Floors Outside the Closet

Water runs out under the closet door and gets beneath carpet pad, laminate or hardwood. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.

Insulation Around the Unit

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Rot in the stand beneath the air handler, which can mean rebuilding it.
  • The water category, because Category 2 needs more cleaning than Category 1.
  • The location of the air handler, since attic, garage and closet leaks spread differently.
  • Whether mold has started and needs containment and removal.

Our Process

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

1

Measure the Spread

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. Then we replace insulation, drywall, baseboard and flooring and repaint so the area matches.
