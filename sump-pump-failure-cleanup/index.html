Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Woodbury: What to Know

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

This water is not clean. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

Your First Steps

  1. Stay Out of the WaterWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
How We Fix It

How We Handle Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

1

Pump Out the Basement

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Wash and Disinfect

An antimicrobial product goes on after the concrete, the studs and any kept belongings have been washed. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. Make sure a functioning pump is installed again by the time drying wraps up.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
  • A power outage during the storm with no battery backup on the pump.
  • A tripped outlet or breaker, or a plug knocked loose, leaving the pump without power.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Framing

The wood plate at the base of each wall sits right on the slab and soaks up water first. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Carpet and Flooring

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

The wet zone inside a wall reaches well past the water mark, as drywall and insulation pull moisture up off the floor. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Outlets and Wiring

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.
