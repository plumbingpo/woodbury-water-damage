Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Woodbury

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Contain the WaterPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingA ceiling that sags with trapped water can be drained: stay off to the side and make a small opening at its lowest spot, with a bucket underneath.
  4. Stay Off the RoofClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. From indoors, mark where the water enters, and let professionals handle everything else.
  5. Document What You SeeBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Corroded or loose metal flashing where the roof meets a chimney, a vent or a wall.
  • Fasteners working loose over time, so water follows the nail shank into the attic.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • Rubber boots around plumbing vent pipes that split after years in the sun.
  • A valley liner that has worn through at the point where the most runoff passes.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Drywall Ceilings

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Roof Framing

Rafters, trusses and decking that get wet again and again begin to darken, swell and soften. Rot in these members weakens the structure that holds the roof up.

Mold in the Attic

A dark, warm attic with damp wood and paper-faced drywall suits mold well. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.

Insulation in the Attic

For days after a leak, matted insulation sits wet on top of the ceiling and no longer insulates. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. A leak put down to an old, worn or neglected roof is usually denied, and fixing the roof may be treated separately from the interior work. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.
